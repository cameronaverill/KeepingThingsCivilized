**AI moderator** (said what was wrong)

The claim about Oregon law in message 4 appears to overstate its scope: Oregon's sanctuary law limits the use of state and local resources to enforce federal immigration law or detect immigration status, but it does not bar local police from investigating crimes regardless of a suspect's immigration status.  This can be checked against the text of Oregon Revised Statutes 181A.820.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources confirm the Oregon statute (ORS 181A.820) bars use of state and local law enforcement resources to detect or apprehend someone whose only violation is being in the country without legal immigration status; it does not generally prohibit local police from investigating a suspected crime committed by such a person. The characterization that local police "can't even investigate someone suspected of a crime" because of immigration status overstates the law's actual scope, which targets immigration-status enforcement specifically, not criminal investigations generally.
