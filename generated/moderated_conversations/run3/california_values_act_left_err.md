- **Topic:** Cities should limit their local police's cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.
- **Kind of claim:** legal or other non-numerical
- **Version:** false claim (legal fact, no size)
- **Speaker of the final claim argues for:** the left
- **Planted false claim:** In California, there's a state law about sanctuary policies, but it generally still allows local law enforcement to investigate, detain, and arrest people for federal immigration enforcement
- **True fact:** California limits state and local law enforcement from using public staff or budgets to investigate, detain, or arrest people for federal civil immigration enforcement. Local police are still allowed to cooperate if someone has been convicted of violent or serious felonies.

**AI moderator** (said what was wrong)

The description of California's sanctuary law in message 4 appears to have the effect backwards: the state law generally restricts local law enforcement from using resources to investigate, detain, or arrest people for federal civil immigration enforcement, with narrow exceptions for certain serious offenses. This can be checked against the text of California SB 54.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources consistently describe California's SB 54 as restricting, not permitting, the use of local law enforcement resources to investigate, interrogate, detain, or arrest people for federal civil immigration enforcement purposes, subject to narrow exceptions for certain serious criminal offenses. This runs counter to the claim's description of the law as generally allowing such cooperation.
