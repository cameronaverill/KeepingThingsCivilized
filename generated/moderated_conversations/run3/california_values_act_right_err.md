The claim in message 4 that local police in California cannot cooperate with federal immigration enforcement even for people convicted of violent crimes appears to overstate the restriction. **AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent summaries of California's SB 54 (the Values Act) indicate it does permit local law enforcement to cooperate with federal immigration authorities in specified circumstances, including when a person has been convicted of serious or violent felonies or certain other enumerated offenses, with cooperation allowed for notification or transfer requests. This suggests the claim that California police cannot cooperate at all for those convicted of violent crimes overstates the law's restriction, which is narrower and includes carve-outs for such convictions.
