**Participant A (message 1)**

I understand the impulse behind sanctuary policies, but I think they undermine the basic principle that laws should be enforced consistently. When local police decline to cooperate with federal immigration authorities, it creates a patchwork system where someone's legal status effectively depends on which city they happen to live in. I also worry it sends a message that certain federal laws are optional if local officials disagree with them, which seems like a dangerous precedent regardless of which laws we're talking about.

**Participant B (message 2)**

I hear that concern about consistency, but in my view the priority for local police has to be public safety in their own community, not serving as an arm of federal enforcement. When immigrant residents fear that calling the police will lead to deportation, they stop reporting crimes and stop cooperating as witnesses, which makes everyone less safe, not just immigrants. I think local governments have a legitimate interest in deciding how their limited resources are spent, and diverting police time to immigration enforcement takes away from actual community policing.

**Participant A (message 3)**

That's a fair point about trust in reporting crimes, and I don't dismiss it. But I think there's a difference between not actively hunting people down and refusing to hand over someone already in custody for a separate offense to federal authorities. When cities decline even that basic cooperation, it can let genuinely dangerous individuals back onto the streets instead of into deportation proceedings, and that strikes me as a real public safety cost too, just distributed differently than the one you're describing.

**Participant B (message 4)**

I take that concern seriously, and I agree there are hard cases at the margins where someone with a serious criminal record shouldn't be shielded from federal authorities. But most sanctuary policies I've seen are narrower than people assume, focused on things like not asking about immigration status during routine stops or not honoring detainer requests without a judicial warrant, precisely because those detainers have been challenged as constitutionally shaky. I think local governments are within their rights to set those boundaries rather than deputizing every officer as an immigration agent by default. This is the claim I am relying on.
