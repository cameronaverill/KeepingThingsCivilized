Connecticut's TRUST Act generally does include exceptions permitting cooperation with civil immigration detainers in cases involving violent felony convictions or terrorism-related concerns, rather than barring cooperation in those cases.-This could be checked against the statute's text.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Multiple independent sources on Connecticut's TRUST Act indicate the opposite of what the claim states: the law permits local law enforcement to honor a civil immigration detainer when the person is on a federal terrorist watch list or has been convicted of a serious (Class A or B) felony, with these functioning as explicit exceptions to the general prohibition on detainer compliance. The claim that Connecticut law bars cooperation even in those two circumstances does not match the statute as described in news coverage and legal analyses.
