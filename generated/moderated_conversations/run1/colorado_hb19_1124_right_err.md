**AI moderator** (asked for a source)

Could a source be given for the claim in message 4 that local law enforcement in Colorado cannot provide federal agents access to non-public jail areas even when a federal judge issues a warrant? Many sanctuary-type restrictions apply specifically to administrative warrants rather than those issued by a judge, so clarifying which type of warrant is meant would help.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Colorado's relevant statute (the 2019 Protect Colorado Residents from Federal Overreach Act, C.R.S. 24-76.6-103) restricts federal immigration access to non-public jail areas, but it carves out an explicit exception for warrants issued by a federal judge or magistrate, or writs from a state or federal judge concerning prisoner transfer. This means the law generally does permit access when a true judicial warrant is presented, contrary to the claim that such access cannot be provided even with a federal judge's warrant; the restrictions instead target administrative warrants and civil immigration detainers, which are not signed by a judge.
