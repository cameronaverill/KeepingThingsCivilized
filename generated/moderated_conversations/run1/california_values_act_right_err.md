**AI moderator** (said what was wrong)

The claim that California law bars local police from cooperating with federal authorities even for violent-crime convictions appears inaccurate. California's sanctuary law (the TRUST Act and related Values Act provisions) generally permits cooperation when someone has been convicted of specified serious or violent offenses; this can be checked against the text of those statutes.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent legal summaries of California's sanctuary law (SB 54 / Values Act, alongside the TRUST Act) indicate it explicitly permits local-federal cooperation when a person has been convicted of specified serious or violent felonies, or certain other enumerated offenses, rather than barring cooperation outright in such cases. This suggests the blanket statement that California police cannot cooperate even with violent-crime convictions does not match the statutory text as described by legal analyses.
