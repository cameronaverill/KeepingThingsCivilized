My worry with sweeping sanctuary policies is that they're a blunt instrument, treating a violent offender the same as a worried parent. Local police already have discretion in plenty of areas; this shouldn't be the one place where federal law gets treated as optional.

**Participant B (message 4)**

But I think the practical reality is that once local police start making exceptions for cooperation, it becomes very hard to draw a clean line that communities can actually trust, so a clearer statewide standard ends up protecting more people without undermining real law enforcement. States have taken different approaches to this exact question, and Oregon is one example worth looking at. This is the claim I am relying on.
