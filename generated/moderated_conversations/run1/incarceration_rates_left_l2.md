I worry that limiting cooperation sends a message that some laws are optional depending on who's affected.

If someone fears that calling the police about a robbery or a domestic assault might lead to their neighbor or even themselves being deported, they simply won't call, and that makes everyone less safe, not more. Local police depend on trust to do their jobs, and I think that trust erodes quickly when officers are seen as an arm of immigration enforcement. I do agree accountability matters, but I think it should be handled by the agencies actually tasked with that job, not by blurring the line with community policing.

**Participant A (message 3)**

I think local governments have an obligation to their citizens to make sure dangerous individuals aren't quietly released back into the community because of a jurisdictional standoff between local and federal authorities. A narrower policy focused only on victims and witnesses seems more defensible to me than the wider sanctuary approach many cities have adopted.

I think it's worth remembering that these are still human beings raising families and working jobs, and treating every unauthorized immigrant as a public safety threat isn't supported by how these communities actually behave day to day. **AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Peer-reviewed and think-tank estimates (notably Cato Institute analyses of Census data) consistently find unauthorized immigrants are incarcerated at lower rates than native-born Americans, but the gap is generally reported around 40 to 50 percent lower, not the 68 to 75 percent figure cited; that larger 66-75 percent range appears in some of the same research but describes legal immigrants' incarceration rate relative to native-born, not unauthorized immigrants. Other studies and arrest-rate analyses (e.g., Texas data) also show lower offending rates for undocumented immigrants, but figures vary by study, year, and crime type, and none reviewed matched the specific 68-75 percent range as stated for unauthorized immigrants specifically.
