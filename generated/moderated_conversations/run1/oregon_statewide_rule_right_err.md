- **Kind of claim:** legal or other non-numerical

- **Speaker of the final claim argues for:** the right
- **Planted false claim:** In Oregon, local law enforcement can't even investigate someone suspected of a crime if they're in the U.S. without legal status
- **True fact:** Oregon holds the nation's oldest statewide rule (passed in 1987), which bans state and local police from using public funds, equipment, or staff to track down or arrest people whose only crime is living in the U.S. without legal status.

**AI moderator** (said what was wrong)

The characterization of Oregon's law in message 4 appears to overstate its effect. Oregon's sanctuary statute generally restricts using state or local resources to detect or apprehend people based solely on immigration status, but it does not bar police from investigating crimes a person is suspected of committing. This distinction may be worth checking against the statute's text.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources on Oregon's sanctuary statute (ORS 181A.820 and related provisions) indicate it restricts state and local law enforcement from using resources to detect or apprehend people whose only violation is being in the country without legal status, but does not bar police from investigating a crime a person is suspected of committing; a City Club legal analysis explicitly states the law does not prohibit an agency from investigating suspected crimes. The claim that Oregon police cannot investigate someone suspected of a crime if undocumented therefore appears to overstate what the statute actually restricts.
