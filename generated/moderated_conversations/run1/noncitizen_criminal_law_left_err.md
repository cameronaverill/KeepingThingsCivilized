**AI moderator** (said what was wrong)

The claim that noncitizens accused of state or local crimes are not released on bail is not accurate as a general statement. Bail decisions for noncitizens vary by jurisdiction and case and are often governed by the same or similar criteria used for citizens, so many noncitizens are in fact granted bail.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources do not support the claim as a categorical statement. In most state and local criminal cases, bail for noncitizen defendants is governed by the same general criteria used for citizens (flight risk, danger to the community, offense severity), and many noncitizens are in fact granted bail; some jurisdictions, such as Louisiana, instead require an added contradictory hearing before setting bail for a noncitizen rather than barring bail outright. Whether a noncitizen actually makes bail or stays detained can also depend on a separate federal immigration detainer, but that is distinct from a blanket rule against bail eligibility in the criminal case itself.
