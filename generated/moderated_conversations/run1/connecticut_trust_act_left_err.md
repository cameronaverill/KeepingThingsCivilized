**AI moderator** (said what was wrong)

The description of Connecticut's sanctuary-type law (often called the TRUST Act) in message 4 may have the effect reversed: that law is generally characterized as restricting local law enforcement cooperation with federal immigration enforcement except in specific circumstances, such as a judicial warrant or certain serious convictions, rather than generally allowing such cooperation. This could be checked against the text of the law or official summaries of it.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources, including Connecticut state guidance and news coverage, describe the Trust Act as generally restricting rather than permitting local law enforcement cooperation with federal civil immigration enforcement; it prohibits detaining or arresting someone solely on an ICE detainer and generally requires a judicial warrant or certain serious convictions before cooperation is allowed. This is the opposite of what the claim describes, which states the law 'generally still allows' such investigation, detention, and arrest.
