**AI moderator** (asked for a source)

Could a source be given for the claim in message 4 that local law enforcement in Illinois cannot hold someone even when a judge signs a warrant? This seems to run counter to how such laws are generally described, where a judicial warrant is often the condition that permits cooperation rather than one that blocks it.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources indicate the Illinois TRUST Act restricts local law enforcement from honoring civil immigration detainers, but it is generally described as permitting cooperation, such as transferring custody or holding someone, when backed by a judicial warrant signed by a judge rather than a mere administrative ICE request. This runs counter to the claim as stated, which suggests a judge's warrant does not enable cooperation; most descriptions of the law treat a judicial warrant as the specific condition under which cooperation is allowed, not blocked.
