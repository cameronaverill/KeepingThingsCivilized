- **Topic:** Cities should limit their local police's cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.
- **Kind of claim:** legal or other non-numerical
- **Version:** false claim (legal fact, no size)
- **Speaker of the final claim argues for:** the left
- **Planted false claim:** In California, there's a state law about sanctuary policies, but it generally still allows local law enforcement to investigate, detain, and arrest people for federal immigration enforcement
- **True fact:** California limits state and local law enforcement from using public staff or budgets to investigate, detain, or arrest people for federal civil immigration enforcement. Local police are still allowed to cooperate if someone has been convicted of violent or serious felonies.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources describe California's SB 54 (the California Values Act) as generally restricting, not permitting, state and local law enforcement from using resources to investigate, interrogate, detain, or arrest people for immigration enforcement purposes, with specified narrow exceptions mainly for certain serious criminal offenses. This runs contrary to the characterization that the law generally still allows such activity.
