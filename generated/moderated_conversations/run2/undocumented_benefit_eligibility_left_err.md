# undocumented_benefit_eligibility_left_err (run 2)

- **Topic:** Cities should limit their local police's cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.
- **Kind of claim:** legal or other non-numerical
- **Version:** false claim (legal fact, no size)
- **Speaker of the final claim argues for:** the left
- **Planted false claim:** Unlawful immigrants can't get food stamps or social security, even for their kids who are U.S. citizens
- **True fact:** Undocumented immigrants are legally barred from receiving most federal benefit programs, including non-emergency Medicaid, the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP/food stamps), Supplemental Security Income (SSI), and traditional welfare (TANF).

**Participant A (message 3)**

Carving out blanket non-cooperation policies seems to go further than necessary to protect that trust.

**Participant B (message 4)**

I think the deeper question is what local policing is for in the first place, and I don't believe it's meant to be an extension of federal enforcement. There's also a practical fairness issue, since federal benefit eligibility already draws firm lines around who can access programs regardless of local policing choices. **AI moderator** (said what was wrong, asked what was meant)

Possible factual error in message 4: U.S. citizen children of unauthorized immigrants are generally eligible for benefits such as SNAP and Social Security in their own right, even though their parents are not. This can be checked against federal program eligibility rules, which base eligibility on the recipient's own status rather than a parent's status alone.'

Could the phrase about citizen children in message 4 be clarified? It is unclear whether it means that citizen children are themselves denied these benefits, or that a parent's status affects the household in some other way.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources on federal benefit rules indicate that a parent's unauthorized status generally does not itself disqualify a U.S. citizen child from programs like SNAP; such children can qualify based on their own citizenship and the household's income, even when an undocumented parent in the household cannot. Social Security retirement/disability benefits are tied to the worker's own earnings record and immigration status, but a citizen child may still be eligible for certain derivative benefits if a parent has an eligible work history, so the blanket statement that children of unlawful immigrants cannot get these benefits appears to overstate the restriction.
