- **Topic:** Cities should limit their local police's cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.
- **Kind of claim:** legal or other non-numerical
- **Version:** false claim (legal fact, no size)
- **Speaker of the final claim argues for:** the left
- **Planted false claim:** In Connecticut, there's a state law about sanctuary policies, but it generally still allows local law enforcement to investigate, detain, and arrest people for federal immigration enforcement. A person doesn't need to be on the terror watch list or be convicted of a felony or anything
- **True fact:** Connecticut bars police and jail staff from honoring civil immigration holds unless the individual is on a terror watch list or has been convicted of a severe violent felony.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent sources, including the statutory text of Connecticut's TRUST Act (Conn. Gen. Stat. \u00a7 54-192h) and official state guidance, indicate the law generally restricts rather than permits local law enforcement from detaining, arresting, or assisting federal authorities on a civil immigration detainer, prohibiting such action unless narrow conditions are met (such as a judicial warrant or certain enumerated serious convictions). This runs contrary to the description that the law 'generally still allows' such detention and arrest without need for a felony conviction or watch-list match.
