**AI moderator** (said what was wrong)

Possible factual error in message 4: California's sanctuary law (SB 54) actually permits local police cooperation with federal immigration enforcement for people convicted of a range of serious and violent offenses, so the claim that cooperation is barred even in those cases does not match the statute's text, which can be checked directly.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Independent summaries of California's SB 54 (the California Values Act) indicate it does permit local law enforcement to notify or transfer individuals to federal immigration authorities, at their discretion, when the person has been convicted of certain serious or violent felonies and a list of other specified offenses (roughly 800 offense categories), with time limits tied to the conviction date. This contradicts a blanket claim that cooperation is barred even for violent-crime convictions; cooperation is restricted and discretionary rather than categorically prohibited for that group.
