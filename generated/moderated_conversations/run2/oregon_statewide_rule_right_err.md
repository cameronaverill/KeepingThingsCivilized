**AI moderator** (said what was wrong)

The claim in message 4 about Oregon law appears to overstate its scope: such laws generally limit using local resources to enforce federal immigration status or detain people solely for immigration violations, not a general bar on investigating crimes committed by people without legal status.  A source for this specific claim could help clarify its actual scope.

**AI moderator: web-search note** (requested after the reply above)

Oregon's sanctuary statute (ORS 181A.820, later strengthened by HB 3265) bars local agencies from using resources to detect or apprehend people whose only violation is being in the country without legal status, and restricts inquiries into immigration status; it does not generally prohibit investigating crimes allegedly committed by people without legal status. The claim as stated appears to describe a broader restriction than what the law's text and official summaries indicate.
