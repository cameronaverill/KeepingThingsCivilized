**Participant A (message 1)**

I think local police cooperating with federal immigration enforcement is just basic law enforcement working as it should. When cities limit that cooperation, they're effectively picking and choosing which federal laws matter, and that undermines the whole system of law. If someone is here illegally and commits a crime, I don't see why local police shouldn't help identify that person for federal authorities. Public safety should come first, and I worry sanctuary policies send the wrong message about accountability.

**Participant B (message 2)**

I understand the accountability concern, but in my view the bigger public safety issue is trust. If immigrant communities believe calling the police means risking deportation for themselves or a neighbor, they stop reporting crimes and stop cooperating as witnesses, which makes everyone less safe. Local police also aren't trained or resourced to enforce federal immigration law, and pulling them into that role distracts from their core job of investigating local crime. I'd rather have police focus on solving crimes than doing the work of a separate federal agency.

**Participant A (message 3)**

That's a fair point about trust, and I won't pretend fear of deportation doesn't affect willingness to come forward. But I think there's a difference between routine policing and cases where someone is already in custody for a serious offense; sharing that information with federal authorities doesn't require going door to door checking papers. My concern is that sanctuary policies can shield repeat offenders who happen to be undocumented, and that seems like an avoidable risk to public safety rather than a trust-building measure. I'd rather see cooperation limited to serious cases than cut off broadly by policy.

**Participant B (message 4)**

I hear that distinction, but even narrowing cooperation to serious cases still requires local police to act as an arm of federal enforcement, which I think blurs a line that protects everyone's rights, citizen and non-citizen alike. Once that door is open even a little, it tends to widen through practice and pressure, and communities notice. I'd rather cities set a clear boundary so residents know local police are there to protect them, not to hand them over. This is the claim I am relying on.
