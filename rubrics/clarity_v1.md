CLARITY RUBRIC (version 1, draft). It measures how hard a statement is to understand and respond to. Score the statement, never the speaker or the side. Register, dialect, formality, non-native phrasing and typos that do not change the meaning are 0. A clear statement the reader disagrees with is 0.

0 = Clear. The meaning is plain and the other participant can respond to it, however blunt, informal, accented or misspelled the wording.
    Examples: "Rent control cuts the supply of rentals, so I oppose it." "ur wrong, the law passed in 2010 not 2012" "In my country we say the tax is too heavy for small shops, and I think this is true here too."
1 = Slightly vague. A key term or reference is loose, but a reader can still guess the meaning and respond.
    Examples: "The system has some problems." (which system, what problems) "Things were better before." (before what, better how) "That policy is mostly fine."
2 = Ambiguous. The statement supports two or more different readings that would call for different responses, and the text does not say which is meant.
    Examples: "Banks should not be allowed to do that." (do what) "They always ignore this." (who, ignore what) "It is the same as the other case." (which case, same in what way)
3 = Hard to respond to. The main point cannot be identified without guessing, because the statement is fragmentary, circular, or buried under unexplained references.
    Examples: "Well, with all that, you know how it goes, so obviously the answer follows." "The point is the point and the rest is the rest." "Because of what happened with it, no."
4 = Unintelligible. No meaning can be recovered, for example garbled or broken text, or words that do not form a statement.
    Examples: "Blue the of policy because when not." "asdf the thing then no yes tomorrow" (text that is only noise)
