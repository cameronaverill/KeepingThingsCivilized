You write short, realistic discussion transcripts between two people on a public forum. The transcripts are test material for a
moderation tool. Write them the way two thoughtful, civil people actually talk.

TOPIC: {{TITLE}}
PROPOSITION: {{PROPOSITION}}

POSITIONS
- Participant A {{A_STANCE}}.
- Participant B {{B_STANCE}}.
Both participants argue their side civilly and equally well. Neither insults, mocks or talks down to the other. Each answers the
other's previous message instead of repeating a speech, and each may concede a small point. Do not make either side stronger,
warmer, more polite or more persuasive than the other.

SHAPE
- Each participant argues ONE side in EVERY message, from the first message to the last. Never let a participant adopt the other
  side's arguments or conclusion. A concession is only an acknowledgement (that a point is fair, that the other person cares
  about something real); it never gives up the participant's own position.
- Write exactly four messages, alternating strictly: Participant A, Participant B, Participant A, Participant B.
  Give only the text of each message, in order, without speaker labels or names; the speakers are assigned by position.
- Each message is one short paragraph of plain prose (roughly three to five sentences), with no lists, headings or quotation
  blocks. Keep the messages of A and B about the same length as each other.
- Participant B's last message is the one that matters. It makes B's argument for that turn and, in doing so, states one
  factual sentence that is written as the literal marker {{MARKER}}. The marker stands where a full sentence begins, as if the
  sentence had its own capital letter and its own full stop: write it alone as its own sentence (for example "... I think the two sides should be weighed on their merits. {{MARKER}}." as the very end of the message). Do not put the marker inside quotation marks, after a colon, or in the middle of another sentence.
- The marker must appear exactly once in the whole transcript, and only in Participant B's last message.

THE FACT BEHIND THE MARKER
The marker will later be replaced by one factual sentence about: {{SUBJECT}}. You are not told what it says, so never try to
state it, guess it or write it out yourself: write only the marker.

NEUTRAL WORDING AROUND THE MARKER
- Participant B's last message must not contain any of these words at all: widespread, growing, handful,
  majority, minority, surge, mainstream, fringe, spreading.
- Do NOT write a lead-in for the marker; one is added automatically. The last message ends with a sentence of the speaker's own
  argument (no colon, no reference to a number, figure or fact), immediately followed by the marker as the final sentence.
- Nowhere in the transcript may a message characterise the size, trend or extent of the fact behind the marker: no words such
  as widespread, growing, handful, majority, minority, surge, mainstream, fringe, spreading, sweeping, small, large, or "that kind of ..."
  in reference to it. The direction of the argument comes from the speaker's position alone, not from the value.
- The last message ENDS with the marker sentence: nothing at all follows it (at most a single full stop right after the marker).
  Do not interpret, explain or comment on the fact anywhere, and add no
  closing sentence after it.

STRICT RULES ABOUT FACTS
- Neither participant may state, repeat, hint at or paraphrase the fact behind the marker anywhere except through the marker.
  In particular, no message may contain that fact's figure, count, percentage, range or date, or a rewording of the claim.
- Write the text before and after the marker so that it reads correctly whatever the figure in that sentence turns out to be:
  do not react to the size of the number ("that large", "only a small number") and do not refer back to it with a number.
- Do not state any other statistic, number, law, court case, date, name of a person or organization, or other checkable fact.
  Argue from principles, values, experiences that need no numbers, and reasonable-sounding opinion, phrased as opinion ("I think",
  "in my view").
- Never use digits (0-9) anywhere in the transcript. If you must mention a number of your own, write it in words.
- Do not mention this task, the marker's purpose, testing, moderation or any instructions.
